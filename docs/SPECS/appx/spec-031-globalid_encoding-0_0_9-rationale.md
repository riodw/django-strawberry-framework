Companion to [`docs/SPECS/spec-031-globalid_encoding-0_0_9.md`][spec-031]. The spec carries the contract; this file carries how the contract was arrived at. Neither duplicates the other — the text here **left** the spec.

Read this when checking a finished implementation against the reasoning that produced it, or before re-opening a settled question. Worker 2 never reads it ([`docs/builder/BUILD.md`][build-md] `### Who reads it, and when`).

**How later passes append to this file.** Each Decision below carries a `### Changes this Decision underwent` section. A reconciliation pass that finds the spec stale against `HEAD` — a renamed symbol, a bound a later card removed, a claim the Decision may no longer make — appends a `**Post-ship:**` bullet there, naming the shipped behavior and the card that changed it. Findings belonging to no single Decision go under [Non-Decision deliberation](#non-decision-deliberation). Nothing needs restructuring to take an addition, and the corrections themselves always land in the spec, stated directly and without chronology.

## Decision 4 — Four strategies (`model`, `type`, `type+model`, callable) and an unchanged `node_id` portion

Spec: [Decision 4 — Four strategies (`model`, `type`, `type+model`, callable) and an unchanged `node_id` portion][spec-031-d4].

### Justification (moved from the spec)

- The four strategies are the card's stated scope. `model` as default is the card's headline; `type` is the parity-preserving opt-out; `type+model` is the explicit transitional mode the card names; callable is the escape hatch for custom schemes.
- Decoupling the type-name slot from the `node_id` slot keeps `GlobalID` filtering, FK-`id` elision, and the relation `id` round-trip untouched ([Current state][spec-031-current-state]).

### Alternatives considered (and rejected)

- **Ship only `model` (no `type` opt-out).** Rejected: the card explicitly keeps the legacy mode available; some consumers need type-scoped identity (disjoint auth/cache scopes), which the card calls out as a "legitimate legacy mode."
- **Make `type` the default and `model` opt-in.** Rejected: the card explicitly makes `model` the new default; deferring the breaking change to opt-in re-creates the post-`1.0.0` migration-work problem the card exists to prevent ([Decision 9](#decision-9--changing-the-default-to-model-is-a-breaking-wire-format-change-acceptable-pre-100)).
- **`type+model` emits type-name during the window.** Rejected: emitting model-anchored from day one is what makes new IDs durable; accepting both on decode is the migration bridge, so the emit direction should already be the target format.

### Changes this Decision underwent

- **Revision 1** pinned the four strategies and made `model` the default.
- **Revision 2 (P1)** changed the callable encoder signature from `(type_cls, model, node_id, info)` to `(type_cls, model, root, info)`. The seam is `resolve_typename(root, info)`, which runs *before* `resolve_id`, so supplying `node_id` would force a premature (and, for an async or custom `resolve_id`, doubled) resolution and contradict the spec's own async edge case. Node-id-dependent type-name slots were scoped out of this seam.
- **Revision 2 (P2)** made `callable` **encode-only** for `0.0.9`. Slice 3 had required a callable decode path the spec left open — an unimplementable contradiction. Round-trip symmetry was narrowed to the three decodable strategies and the open question closed.
- **Revision 3 (P2)** pinned the installed `callable` closure to raise [`ConfigurationError`][glossary-configurationerror] on a non-`str` / empty return rather than letting Strawberry's `Node._id` assertion fire.
- **Revision 3 (P1)** added the fifth effective strategy `custom` (a preserved consumer `resolve_typename` override), a sibling of `callable` and likewise encode-only.
- **Revision 7 delta (b)** narrowed the callable signature again, to `(type_cls, model, root) -> str`: `info` was dropped. The type-name slot is a durable identity component, so the encoder must be a pure function of the row's identity; withholding `info` makes that purity a consumer obligation rather than an opening to fold request-scoped state into a durable ID. Breaking, pre-`1.0`.
- **Claim retired.** The Decision may no longer say the callable encoder receives `info` (Revision 2's spelling) or `node_id` (Revision 1's spelling). The only supported signature is `(type_cls, model, root) -> str`, sync.
- **Post-ship (`031` residual cycle, Slice 2) — `isinstance(result, str)` is not a `str`.** The Decision described one per-call return check, a non-empty-`str` guard, and stopped there. Shipped [`types/relay.py::encode_typename`][relay] adds a second step: the validated return is re-read through the base `str.__str__` descriptor and the *normalized* value is what the emptiness check runs on and what the encoder returns. The class it closes is a `str` **subclass** that passes `isinstance` and then overrides `__str__` / `__format__` to raise or to substitute a different payload once Strawberry's base64 encoder reads it — a value that satisfied the contract at the check and violated it at the use. The principle is the sibling of Slice 1's un-inspectable-callable rule: **a validated value must be inert by the time it leaves the validator, not merely well-typed at the moment it is inspected.** Pinned by `tests/types/test_relay_interfaces.py::test_globalid_callable_string_subclass_is_normalized` (and the `""` row of `::test_globalid_callable_non_string_return_raises`). The spec's Decision 4 and the Slice-2 test list now state it. No behavior the Decision described changed; the Decision was short of what shipped.

## Decision 5 — Precedence: `Meta.globalid_strategy` → `RELAY_GLOBALID_STRATEGY` → package default (`model`)

Spec: [Decision 5 — Precedence: `Meta.globalid_strategy` → `RELAY_GLOBALID_STRATEGY` → package default (`model`)][spec-031-d5].

### Justification (moved from the spec)

- `Meta`-over-setting-over-default is the package's standard precedence shape (the card pins it verbatim) and matches how a per-type opt-out should beat a project-wide knob.
- Resolving at finalization (not per request) means the injected `resolve_typename` is a fixed closure — no per-call strategy lookup on the `id`-resolution hot path, and a single source of truth for the type's identity format.

### Alternatives considered (and rejected)

- **Resolve per request.** Rejected: the `GlobalID` format is a durable client contract; varying it per request would mint inconsistent IDs and defeat caching.
- **Setting-only (no per-type override).** Rejected: the card wants per-type `Meta.globalid_strategy` so a consumer can keep one type type-scoped while the rest go model-anchored (disjoint auth/cache scopes).

### Changes this Decision underwent

- **Revision 1** pinned `Meta` → setting → `"model"` precedence and froze the resolution at finalization.
- **Revision 3 (P1)** layered the override classification on top: a preserved consumer `resolve_typename` override forces `custom` regardless of the precedence, and declaring both an override and an explicit `Meta.globalid_strategy` raises.
- **Revision 7 correction** named the reader: the setting is read through [`conf.py::relay_globalid_strategy_setting()`][conf], a `getattr(settings, "RELAY_GLOBALID_STRATEGY", None)` helper delivering "absent → default", **not** a bare `conf.settings.RELAY_GLOBALID_STRATEGY` attribute access, which would raise `AttributeError` on the missing key.
- **Revision 7 delta (a)** replaced the lazy read with a once-per-registry-lifecycle snapshot. The pre-hardening one-arg resolver validated `RELAY_GLOBALID_STRATEGY` only on the branch a type actually fell through to, so a schema with zero Relay-Node types — or one where every type declares its own strategy or overrides `resolve_typename` — could ship an invalid setting. The validated value is now computed unconditionally before the Relay loop, stored on `registry._globalid_setting_snapshot`, and passed into a **pure two-arg** `_resolve_globalid_strategy(definition, globalid_setting)` that reads no setting and validates nothing. A mid-lifecycle setting change detected on a finalization retry raises [`ConfigurationError`][glossary-configurationerror] naming `registry.clear()`.
- **Claims retired.** The Decision may no longer describe a one-argument `_resolve_globalid_strategy` that reads and validates the setting itself, nor a bare `conf.settings.RELAY_GLOBALID_STRATEGY` access.
- **Post-ship (`031` residual cycle, Slice 1).** The snapshot slot's un-stamped state is the [`registry.py::GLOBALID_SETTING_UNSET`][registry] sentinel, not `None`, and the Decision never named it. The distinction is load-bearing rather than cosmetic: an *absent* `RELAY_GLOBALID_STRATEGY` validates to `None`, so a `None`-initialized slot would make "no snapshot taken yet" and "snapshot taken, no override configured" indistinguishable — the finalizer would re-stamp on every retry and the mid-lifecycle-change guard would never fire for a project that had no setting at first finalize and added one before the retry. `registry.clear()` resets the slot to the sentinel, not to `None`. The spec's Decision 5, Decision 7, the Slice-1 checklist, the error shapes, and DoD item 3 now name it. No behavior changed; the shipped code has carried the sentinel since the delta-(a) snapshot work, and only the spec was short of it.
- **Post-ship (`031` residual cycle, Slice 1).** The `"model"` package default is the named [`types/base.py::DEFAULT_GLOBALID_STRATEGY`][base] constant that `_resolve_globalid_strategy` imports, not a re-typed literal; the Decision now names it so the tier-3 contract is locatable from the spec.

- **Revision 1** pinned the net-new `ALLOWED_META_KEYS` entry and the definition slot, reusing the [`spec-030`][spec-030] [Decision 8][spec-030] / [`spec-029`][spec-029] [Decision 6][spec-029] precedent.
- **Revision 4 (P2)** moved callable arity and sync-ness validation to type creation via `inspect.signature` and `iscoroutinefunction`, so the opaque-`TypeError`-at-request-time class of failure joined the build-time fail-loud posture. The per-call non-`str` return guard stayed in the installed closure, being the one check that can only run per call.

The `inspect.signature(...).bind(...)` arity check was unchanged.

Shipped [`types/base.py::_validate_globalid_callable`][base] wraps **both** probes: a `BaseException` from `is_async_callable` and a `BaseException` from `inspect.signature(...).bind(...)` (beyond the ordinary `TypeError` / `ValueError` arity arm) each become the same [`ConfigurationError`][glossary-configurationerror] naming the expected `(type_cls, model, root) -> str` shape. - **Post-ship (`031` residual cycle, Slice 1) — the dropped-`info` four-arg encoder is a named rejection.** [Decision 4](#decision-4--four-strategies-model-type-typemodel-callable-and-an-unchanged-node_id-portion)'s Rev-7 delta (b) removed `info` from the callable signature, which makes the pre-`0.0.9` `(type_cls, model, root, info)` encoder a wrong-arity value the three-arg bind rejects. `tests/types/test_base.py::test_meta_globalid_strategy_callable_old_four_arg_signature_rejected` pins that consequence, and the spec's Decision 6 and Slice-1 test list now name it rather than leaving it an unowned test.
- **Post-ship (`031` residual cycle, Slice 5) — the four-arg shape was dated wrong, and it was wrong on its own date.** The bullet above, and the Decision 6 sentence it produced, called `(type_cls, model, root, info)` "the pre-`0.0.9`" encoder. It is not: [`CHANGELOG.md`][changelog]'s `## [0.0.9]` `### Added` bullet ships the callable as `(type_cls, model, root, info) -> str`, and the drop of `info` is recorded as a breaking change under `## [0.0.14]` `### Changed`. The lesson is the fourth stale-sentence case in the small: **a dated claim written during reconciliation is not protected by the reconciliation** — this one was authored by this cycle and graded by nobody, because a slice checks the sentences it inherits and not the ones it writes.

## Decision 8 — Decode routes through Django's app registry then the framework registry to the primary type

- Django's `apps.get_model` is the canonical `app_label.model → model` resolver and the durable anchor the whole card is built on; routing through it (then `registry.get`) reuses the registry's existing primary-resolution contract rather than inventing a parallel label index.
- Primary-routing matches how relation targets already resolve ([`DjangoTypeDefinition.related_target_for`][definition] uses `registry.get`), so a decoded ID and a traversed relation land on the same type.
- Keying type-name decode on `graphql_type_name` (not `type_cls.__name__`) is the only spelling that round-trips a `type`-strategy ID: the encode side emits `graphql_type_name`, so the decode side must invert the same function or a [`Meta.name`][glossary-metaname] rename silently breaks refetch.
- Resolving the candidate's strategy and enforcing the payload shape (Step 2) is what gives each strategy its documented contract — without it the decoder is permissive-by-default and `model` / `type` / `type+model` collapse into one indistinguishable behavior.

### Alternatives considered (and rejected)

- **Decode to a secondary type.** Rejected: ambiguous which secondary; the primary is the canonical relation-resolution target, and `type`-scoped IDs are the explicit path for disjoint scopes.
- **A package-owned `{label: type}` index instead of Django's app registry.** Rejected: duplicates Django's model registry, misses proxy/MTI label resolution Django already handles, and drifts on app renames.
- **Reuse Strawberry's native `GlobalID.resolve_type`.** Rejected: it is hardcoded to `info.schema.get_type_by_name(type_name)` (a GraphQL type name), so it cannot resolve a model label; the package must own the model-label dispatch.
- **A permissive decoder that accepts any resolvable shape (no Step-2 strategy enforcement).** Rejected (review finding P1): it makes `type+model` indistinguishable from the default, lets a `model`-strategy type keep accepting stale type-anchored IDs, and dissolves the `type` strategy's type-scoped guarantee.
- **Type-name decode via `type_cls.__name__`.** Rejected (review finding P1): the `type` strategy emits `graphql_type_name`, so a `Meta.name`-renamed type would emit its `Meta.name` value yet be undecodable by class name; decode must invert the encode function.
- **Model-label-routing invariant via option 2 — authorize a model-label payload's shape from the *set* of all registered definitions for the model (while still routing to the primary).** Rejected (review finding P1) in favor of the stricter option 1 (the finalization invariant): option 2 leaves the schema in a state where a model-label ID is *accepted* but its resolved row comes from the primary's `get_queryset`, not the emitter's — a subtler identity confusion than a loud finalization error, and harder to reason about. Option 1 makes the misconfiguration impossible to ship.

### Changes this Decision underwent

- **Revision 1** pinned the app-registry-then-framework-registry dispatch to the primary type.
- **Revision 2 (P1)** rewrote the dispatch as a two-step **resolve-then-enforce**: resolve a candidate, then reject a payload shape the candidate's strategy does not emit. Without Step 2, `type+model` was indistinguishable from a permissive decoder and `model` kept silently accepting stale type-anchored IDs.
- **Revision 2 (P1)** keyed type-name decode on `graphql_type_name`, not `type_cls.__name__`: a [`Meta.name`][glossary-metaname]-renamed type emits its `graphql_type_name`, so decode must invert the same function. The new `registry.definition_for_graphql_name(name)` helper landed with it.

## Decision 10 — `resolve_typename` injection via the `__func__` identity test, at Phase 2.5

Spec: [Decision 10 — `resolve_typename` injection via the `__func__` identity test, at Phase 2.5][spec-031-d10].

### Justification (moved from the spec)

- Phase 2.5 is where Relay resolver injection already happens, and the `__func__` identity test is the shipped, tested mechanism for preserving consumer overrides; reusing both keeps one source of truth for Relay-method injection.
- Keeping `resolve_typename` out of `_RELAY_RESOLVER_DEFAULTS` respects that table's invariant (name → one static default) while still installing through the same `__func__`-gated discipline.
- Classifying an override as `custom`/encode-only (rather than letting the override emit while the type's `Meta`/default strategy drives decode) is the only rule that keeps the package's emit and decode in agreement — the framework never emits an ID its own decoder refuses, because for a `custom` type the decoder explicitly declines (encode-only), exactly as for `callable`.
- Recording the effective strategy at finalization (pre-install) is required precisely because installing the framework closure erases the `__func__` signal decode would otherwise need.

### Alternatives considered (and rejected)

- **Add `resolve_typename` to `_RELAY_RESOLVER_DEFAULTS`.** Rejected: that table's entries are static `(name, default_impl)` pairs; the typename default varies by resolved strategy, so it cannot be a single static impl.
- **Inject at class-creation time (`__init_subclass__`) instead of Phase 2.5.** Rejected: the effective strategy depends on the `RELAY_GLOBALID_STRATEGY` setting and the registry being stable; finalization is the package's "registry is stable now" point, matching where the other Relay defaults install. (The MRO-aware `__func__` override test also belongs here — an override inherited from a non-`Meta` base is invisible to a `cls.__dict__`-only check at class creation.)
- **Let a `resolve_typename` override silently win and the `Meta.globalid_strategy` drive decode anyway.** Rejected (review finding P1): that is the rev2 self-inconsistency — the override emits a label the (`model`-default) decoder then rejects. The two are contradictory; the override wins for encode and forces `custom`/encode-only decode, and declaring both explicitly fails loud.

### Changes this Decision underwent

- **Revision 1** pinned the Phase-2.5 injection and the `__func__` identity test reused from the `0.0.5` Relay foundation.
- **Revision 3 (P1)** classified a preserved consumer override as the fifth effective strategy `custom`, recorded the resolved effective strategy on the [`DjangoTypeDefinition`][definition] at finalization, and made the both-declared case (override plus an explicit `Meta.globalid_strategy`) raise. The recording is required because the post-install `__func__` can no longer distinguish an override from the framework closure.
- **Revision 4 (P1)** added the **step-0 re-entrancy guard**. `finalize_django_types` is documented to be re-runnable after a mid-Phase-2.5 raise (the per-entry `finalized` flags flip only in Phase 3), and the model-label-routing audit is exactly such an in-phase raise. Without the guard a re-run would re-run the `__func__` test against the type's own installed framework closure, misclassify it `custom`, and silently break decode on the recoverable path the finalizer promises to support.
- **Revision 4 (P2)** named the recorded field `effective_globalid_strategy: str | None = None`, distinct from the raw class-creation-time `globalid_strategy` slot: a raw callable value and the resolved `custom` classification are different things.
- **Revision 6 (P1)** hardened the override-detection seam against concrete inheritance, reproduced with a runnable script against the fakeshop models. A concrete Relay `DjangoType` subclassing another concrete Relay `DjangoType` — the natural `Meta.primary`-secondary shape — inherited the parent's installed closure through the MRO; the bare `__func__` test classified it a consumer override, so the child was silently recorded `custom` (encode-only, routing-audit-blind), a child declaring a legitimate `Meta.globalid_strategy` tripped a spurious both-declared [`ConfigurationError`][glossary-configurationerror], and the inherited closure kept emitting the *parent's* payload because it had captured the parent's `definition`. The root fix has three parts: `_install_typename_closure` stamps every framework closure with the `_FRAMEWORK_CLOSURE_MARKER` sentinel (on the plain function, so it survives `classmethod.__func__` retrieval); `_consumer_overrode_resolve_typename` treats a marked function as **not** a consumer override; and the `type` classification gains the **shadow-install**, so a `type`-strategy child inheriting a marked closure installs its own rather than letting the parent's captured definition keep shadowing Strawberry's default. The discrimination is transitive. An unmarked override inherited from an abstract base still classifies `custom` — the intended semantics.
- **Revision 6 (P1), consequence.** The shadow-install made `encode_typename`'s `type` branch the live implementation for exactly that shape; before it, that branch was production-dead.
- **Revision 7 delta (a)** threaded the validated setting snapshot into the install step as the `globalid_setting` argument — see [Decision 5](#decision-5--precedence-metaglobalid_strategy--relay_globalid_strategy--package-default-model).
- **Claims retired.** The Decision may no longer describe the override test as a bare `__func__` identity comparison with no framework-closure exclusion, and may no longer call `encode_typename`'s `type` branch production-dead.
- **Post-ship (`031` residual cycle, Slice 2) — "the one live production path" was an exclusivity claim, and `032` falsified it.** Revision 6's consequence line was written when the shadow-install was the only caller able to reach `encode_typename`'s `type` branch. [`DONE-032-0.0.9`][kanban] then shipped the public `testing/relay.py::global_id_for` helper, which reads a type's recorded `effective_globalid_strategy` and calls `encode_typename` directly — for a `type`-strategy type that is a second live entry into the same branch, with no installed closure involved. The Decision now states the part that is still true and load-bearing (the shadow-install is the only way a `type`-strategy type carries a framework closure, hence the only route into that branch during `id` resolution) and names the helper as the other caller. The general shape: **an exclusivity claim about who reaches a code path is a claim about the whole package at one moment, and a later card adding a caller falsifies it without touching the file the claim is about.** A membership claim ("this is how X gets a closure") survives; a census claim ("this is the only path") does not.
- **Post-ship (`031` residual cycle, Slice 2) — the collapse warning had no owning sentence.** Shipped [`types/finalizer.py::_warn_model_label_secondary_collapse`][finalizer] runs immediately after the model-label-routing audit, over the same materialized multi-type-model tuple, and warns (never raises) when a NON-primary registered type also emits model-label IDs: its `GlobalID`s decode through `registry.get(model)` to the primary, so the secondary refetches AS the primary and its distinct identity and `get_queryset` visibility scope collapse into the primary's. That is legal under Decision 8 (a model-anchored ID cannot distinguish secondaries) and is precisely why the hard audit permits it — but it is also a behavior regression against the pre-`0.0.9` type-name default, under which those two types had distinct self-routing IDs, and it is the most likely accidental configuration now that `model` is the default. Nothing in the spec contracted it: the Decisions covered the raise and were silent on the warn. Pinned by `tests/types/test_relay_interfaces.py::test_model_label_secondary_collapse_warns_and_routes_to_primary` and `::test_model_label_no_collapse_warning_when_secondary_is_type`. The spec's Decision 10, the Slice-2 checklist, the implementation-plan row, DoD item 4, and the Slice-2 test list now state it. Recorded alongside it: `_format_model_label_routing_error`'s remediation branches on whether the primary's recorded strategy is a string or `None`, because [`Meta.globalid_strategy`][glossary-djangotype] is rejected on a non-Relay type and the default fix sentence would otherwise prescribe an impossible one.

## Decision 13 — `GlobalID` filter validation is strategy-aware

Spec: [Decision 13 — `GlobalID` filter validation is strategy-aware][spec-031-d13].

### Justification (moved from the spec)

- For the three framework strategies the `type_name` check is a defense-in-depth sanity guard, so making it strategy-aware keeps the guard where the label can be computed and never blocks a legitimate emitted ID. For `callable` / `custom` the framework cannot compute the expected label AND has no decode path, so the correct posture is to fail closed rather than degrade to node-id-only matching: an encode-only target's IDs are not framework-consumable, and accepting them on `node_id` alone silently admitted arbitrary payloads (Revision 7 delta (c)).
- Reading the recorded `effective_globalid_strategy` keeps the filter layer on the single durable contract decode already uses — no second source of truth, no per-request settings read.
- Rejecting at schema build (the [`_audit_globalid_filter_strategies`][finalizer] Phase-2.5 audit) surfaces the misconfiguration at the earliest fail-loud point; the runtime `GLOBALID_UNVALIDATABLE` `GraphQLError` in [`_decode_and_validate_global_id`][filters-base] only backstops hand-built filtersets that never went through binding.

### Alternatives considered (and rejected)

- **Leave the filter check on `graphql_type_name` and tell consumers to filter with type-anchored IDs.** Rejected: the emitted ID is model-anchored under the default, so the consumer would have to hand-translate every ID before filtering — exactly the boilerplate the package removes, and a silent regression of an already-shipped surface.
- **Drop the `type_name` guard entirely (node-id-only for all strategies).** Rejected: it discards a useful wrong-model rejection the three framework strategies can still enforce.
- **Fall back to node-id-only for `callable` / `custom`** (the Revision 5 → `0.0.13` shipped behavior, replaced by the `0.0.14` hardening — Rev 7 delta (c)). Rejected: an encode-only strategy has no framework decode path, so a decode-requiring `GlobalID` input can never be validly consumed against such a target; node-id-only matching silently accepted arbitrary payloads. Fail-closed (build-time [`ConfigurationError`][glossary-configurationerror] from [`_audit_globalid_filter_strategies`][finalizer] plus the runtime `GLOBALID_UNVALIDATABLE` backstop) is the correct posture; a consumer who genuinely needs to filter a `callable` / `custom` type owns a decode path (or awaits the root-Node decode in [`DONE-032-0.0.9`][kanban]). The one retained node-id-only path is the unbound-owner / unresolvable-target `None`-expected case, where no strategy is available at all.

### Changes this Decision underwent

- **Revision 5 (P1) created this Decision.** Every earlier revision claimed the default flip touched only the `node_id` slot and that `GlobalID` filtering was therefore transparent to it. That was false: [`filters/base.py::_decode_and_validate_global_id`][filters-base] validates the whole `type_name` against the owner / target `graphql_type_name`, so an emitted `products.item:<pk>` would be rejected by a `model`-strategy filter — a silent regression of the card's own headline workflow. The new Decision made that validation strategy-aware and co-landed it with the flip in Slice 2; the false "filtering unchanged" wording was corrected in Current state, Goals, the edge cases, Slice 4, and the Test plan.
- **Revision 5** specified a node-id-only fallback for a `callable` / `custom` target: decode, take `node_id`, skip the `type_name` guard. That is what shipped through `0.0.13`.
- **Revision 7 delta (c)** replaced the fallback with fail-closed behavior, the shipped `0.0.14` hardening. A node-id-only match silently accepts any payload for an encode-only strategy that has no framework decode path. A [`GlobalID`][glossary-relay-node-integration] filter bound against a `callable` / `custom`-strategy target now raises [`ConfigurationError`][glossary-configurationerror] at schema build from the Phase-2.5 [`types/finalizer.py::_audit_globalid_filter_strategies`][finalizer] audit run inside `_bind_filtersets`, with a runtime `GraphQLError` carrying `extensions.code = "GLOBALID_UNVALIDATABLE"` from [`filters/base.py::_decode_and_validate_global_id`][filters-base] backstopping any hand-built filterset that reaches request time. A resolved target whose `effective_globalid_strategy` is a known `None` is rejected the same way. The one surviving node-id-only path is the unbound-owner / unresolvable-target case, where no definition — and so no strategy — is available at all.
- **Claim retired.** The Decision may no longer offer a node-id-only fallback for `callable` / `custom` targets.
- **Post-ship (`031` residual cycle, Slice 2) — "rejected the same way" collapsed two different sites into one.** Rev-7 delta (c) described the fail-closed posture as a build-time audit plus a runtime backstop and then said a known-`None`-strategy target is "rejected the same way". It is not. Shipped [`types/finalizer.py::_audit_globalid_filter_strategies`][finalizer] keys on `ENCODE_ONLY_GLOBALID_STRATEGIES` — the two literal names `callable` / `custom` — so a target whose recorded strategy is `None` passes the build-time audit; only [`filters/base.py::_decode_and_validate_global_id`][filters-base], which keys on the complementary `strategy not in FRAMEWORK_GLOBALID_STRATEGIES`, rejects it, at request time. The two guards are deliberately spelled from opposite directions and cover different sets, and the shipped split is right: a build-time raise on a `None` strategy would reject a `GlobalID` filter on any not-yet-Relay-shaped target during finalization, where `None` is also the ordinary pre-install state. The retired claim: the Decision, the error shapes, the edge cases, the Slice-2 checklist, and DoD item 4 may no longer say a known-`None` target is rejected at schema build. All five now state the runtime-only rejection. The general shape: **"the same way" is a claim about two guards' predicates, and predicates written from opposite directions are the case where it is most likely to be wrong.**
- **Post-ship (`031` residual cycle, Slice 2) — the audit and the runtime path share one target resolver.** The Decision described the two guards without saying they resolve the same target. Shipped [`filters/base.py::resolve_globalid_target_definition`][filters-base] is that single site: the own-PK-vs-relation (and multi-hop) decision factored out of the runtime `_target_definition_for` accessor so the build-time audit resolves from `filterset_cls._owner_definition` + `filter.field_name` exactly what the runtime resolves from a live filter instance. Without it the audit and the backstop could validate against different definitions and disagree about whether a binding is legal. The spec's Decision 13 and the Slice-2 checklist now name it. The `## Current state` sentence naming `_expected_global_id_type_name` as the live helper is **not** drift and is untouched: that helper was live at the spec's authoring commit `b1f82f0e` (verified read-only via `git show b1f82f0e:django_strawberry_framework/filters/base.py`), and `## Current state` is a licensed dated observation of the pre-build repo.
- **Post-ship (`031` residual cycle, Slice 4) — two of this Decision's three filter paths are live-covered by the migration bullet, not by a named test.** The Slice-4 [Test plan][spec-031-test-plan] named only the own-PK `exact` round-trip (`test_globalid_filter_round_trip`), while the Decision contracts `GlobalIDFilter`, `GlobalIDMultipleChoiceFilter`, and [`RelatedFilter`][glossary-relatedfilter]-expanded child filters as one shared code path.
